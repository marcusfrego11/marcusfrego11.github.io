Projects & Repairs

Ibanez TMB100

Completed: 08/26

When the bass first came to me, two of its tuners were damaged, its neck was dried out with a stripped truss rod nut, and its dual-concentric potentiometers were broken off and not functioning properly.

- I was able to replace the tuners with a brand-new set of four Fender-style tuners, flipping those that pointed right instead of the Fender-usual left.

- I re-humidified the neck through intensive moisture treatment for over 48 hours before installing a brand-new truss rod nut (thankfully this style of nut is replacable. I then filed and polished its frets, as per usual with guitars and basses I repair.)

- After discovering that OEM replacement parts for this guitar were extremely expensive, I decided to ditch its original wiring and active pre-amp for a more traditional J Bass style wiring. I drilled a third hole in its pickguard, installed brand-new Alpha pots, and wired it up following regular J Bass wiring.

Squire J Bass

Completed: 09/26

This bass was missing a pickguard, three tuners, and its potentiometers were wired incorrectly, rendering the instrument non-functional.

- I was able to replace the missing tuners with brand-new Fender tuners.

- I rewired its potentiometers and installed a brand-new pickguard.

Fender Rumble 15 (2011)

Completed: 10/26

This amp's power LED turned on but did not output any sound or produce any noise.

- I was able to replace a faulty resistor on the amp that fixed all aforementioned issues.

Fender Rumble 350 (2010)

Completed: 11/26

This amp turned on and produced noise but provided no signal when an instrument was plugged in. Its vinyl wrapping was also peeling at the back and its castor wheels missing all of their bearings.

- After simply cleaning the contacts of its input and effects loop jacks, the amp functioned properly.

- I replaced the amp's castor wheels with brand-new locking ones and glued the amp's wrapping back together with wood glue.

Epiphone Les Paul Classic (2021)

Honeyburst

Acquired: ??

My very first guitar. Despite its abuse (note the nickel-sized chip in the body), it remains a favourite guitar of mine. It is a versatile instrument that has served me well playing in Jazz Band as well as providing authentic tones for the 70s-90s rock I often find myself playing.

Modifications & Specifications

12/2025: Upgraded stock quick-connector wiring harness to a ToneShaper wiring kit with push-pull coil split pots.

08/2026: Switched knobs to goldtops for the rhythm controls and two-tone knobs for the treble controls, mirroring that of Alex Lifeson's "Honey" Les Paul. While solely aesthetic, it is one of my favorite visual touches of the guitar.

Dunlop GCB-95 Cry Baby Wah (1988)

Acquired: 05/2026

A classic wah pedal with a few tasteful mods.

Modifications & Setup

While it began life as a bone-stock late-80s unit, this pedal took less than an hour in my possesion to recieve significant modifications.

05/2026: Upgraded its inductor to a Jim Dunlop Red Fasel and changed capacitor and resistor values to mirror vintage Vox units.

09/2026: Removed stock rubber stoppers for greater physical sweep of the pedal and a more smooth motion.

Boss DD-3 Digital Delay (2000)

Acquired: 05/2026

A reliable and versatile digital delay pedal with a wide range of delay times and effects. Despite its age and lack of features compared to modern Boss releases such as the DD-3T and DD-8, I still find it to be a valuable addition to my setup.

Modifications & Setup

Being an older unit, its through-hole design allows for easier modifications that modern SMD designs do not.

05/2026: Soldered a 0.22μF capacitor to pins 1 and 3 of the feedback potentiometer to roll off harsh high-end and produce a more "analog-like" sound.
